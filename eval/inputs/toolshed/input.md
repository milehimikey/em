# Toolshed — community tool library (PRD)

A neighbourhood runs a shared tool shed. A shed manager stocks it; neighbors join, borrow tools, hold tools for pickup, and get chased when a loan runs late. Written as the business sees it, one story at a time: stock the shed, join the shed, borrow and return a tool, chase an overdue loan, hold a tool for pickup, keep late returners in check, take a tool or a neighbor out of the shed.

Out of scope: payments, fines, reservations by date, a second shed.

Note for the eval (GH #423): this input was reconstructed from the toolshed-v2 model's slice docs (their Intent and Trigger sections, per story), so step names echo the reference model; naming metrics do not apply to this input — the line-shape metrics (`todoInboundLines`, `storylineReadsWithoutAfter`) do.

## Stock the shed

- **Add Tool.** Put a tool into the shed so neighbors can borrow it. The shed manager records the tool's name, an optional description, and its default checkout period. That period is the longest period a checkout of the tool can start with. A tool can be borrowed as soon as it is added: the Tool Catalog lists it as available , and Check Out Tool lends it. The shed manager, from the Tool Inventory screen, when a new physical item joins the shed. Each physical item is added on its own, including an item identical to one already in the shed.

- **Tool Inventory.** Shows the shed manager every tool in the shed on the Tool Inventory screen, and which of them are out on loan, to whom, and until when. It is the shed manager's working screen for tools: they add and retire tools from it, and record a tool lost when it is out and never coming back. - Who: the shed manager, opening the Tool Inventory screen. - It shows: one entry per tool currently in the shed or out on loan, built from the facts above. Nothing on this screen records anything by itself; its actions issue commands owned by other steps: - **Add Tool** — always offered. - **Retire** — offered on every listed tool that is not already retired . - **Mark lost** — offered only on a tool that is out on loan .

- **Tool Catalog.** Shows neighbors every tool they can borrow or hold, on the Tool Catalog screen, and what state each one is in: available, checked out (and until when), reserved for the hold queue, or set aside for a holder. It is the neighbors' working screen for tools: they check tools out and place holds from it. - Who: a neighbor, opening the Tool Catalog screen. - It shows: one entry per tool that is in the shed or out on loan and not retired, built from the facts above. Every neighbor sees exactly the same catalog . Nothing on this screen records anything by itself; its actions issue commands owned by other steps: - **Check Out** — offered by the tool's state alone . - **Place Hold** — offered by the tool's state alone .


## Join the shed

- **Register Neighbor.** Let a neighbor join the tool shed by signing themselves up, so they can borrow tools. Signing up records the neighbor's name and the email address the shed uses to reach them. A new neighbor is active and can borrow straight away. A person who wants to join, from the Sign Up screen. They sign themselves up; the shed manager doesn't enroll neighbors. Someone whose earlier account is closed may sign up again here, as a new account. Once signed up, the neighbor signs in outside the model, and sign-in supplies their neighbor ID for every later action; it resolves a person to their newest account, or to their closed one only when they have no newer.

- **Neighbors.** Shows the shed manager every neighbor account that has signed up, with each account's current status (active, suspended or closed), on the Neighbors screen. It is the shed manager's one screen for neighbors: it shows who the system has flagged for repeated late returns and the late returns behind each flag, who is suspended and since when, and account closures, and it is the screen the shed manager suspends and reinstates neighbors from. The system flags; the shed manager decides. Version 2 folds the former Flagged Neighbors and Suspended Neighbors views into this one: both were filters of the same accounts, and each offered one action this screen now offers. - Who: the shed manager, opening the Neighbors screen. - It shows: one entry per account ever registered, built from the facts above. Nothing on this screen records anything by itself; its actions issue commands owned by other steps: - **Suspend** — offered on every active account, flagged or not . - **Reinstate** — offered on every suspended account . - Flagging is done by the system (Flag Neighbor). A neighbor is not told they were flagged, and a suspended neighbor is never flagged (Neighbors To Flag, a rule). No email goes to the neighbor on suspension or reinstatement, and no reason or note is recorded with a suspension. - Showing only the flagged accounts, or only the suspended ones, is the screen's own filter over this one list; there is no separate the facts above for either.

- **My Account.** Shows a neighbor their own account on the My Account screen: their name, their current email, and whether the account is active, suspended or closed. It is where a neighbor learns they are suspended, and the screen they change their email and close their account from. - Who: a neighbor, opening their own My Account screen. A neighbor whose account is closed can still open it, read-only. - **Whose account it shows:** the account of the neighbor asking. Sign-in, handled outside the model, supplies the neighbor's ID, resolving a person to their newest account, or to their closed one only when they have no newer. - **Actions offered:** nothing on this screen records anything by itself. Its actions issue commands owned by other steps: - **Change Email** — offered while the account isn't closed . - **Close Account** — offered while the account isn't closed .

- **Change Email.** Let a neighbor change the email address the shed uses for them, from My Account, so a wrong or outdated address can be put right and overdue and pickup emails reach them. The new address takes effect at once, and the old one is free for anyone to use. A neighbor, from their own My Account screen, for their own account. My Account offers Change Email on any account that isn't closed ; a suspended neighbor is offered it and refused with a reason they can see. The shed manager doesn't change neighbors' emails.


## Borrow and return a tool

- **Check Out Tool.** Let a neighbor borrow a tool, self-service, from the Tool Catalog. The checkout opens a loan with a due date set from the tool's default checkout period, or from a shorter period the neighbor chooses. It is also how a holder collects a tool set aside for them: there is no separate pickup command. The checkout records only Tool Checked Out; the hold's end is recorded by the Hold Collector (Collect Hold), which reacts to the checkout. The neighbor who will borrow the tool, from the Tool Catalog screen, on a tool the catalog offers Check Out on (available, reserved or set aside). The shed manager does not record checkouts. Sign-in, handled outside the shed's events, supplies the acting neighbor's ID.

- **My Loans.** Shows a neighbor the tools they have out right now, when each is due back, and anything that stands in the way of keeping a tool longer: the loan is overdue, someone else is waiting for the tool, or the tool has been retired. It also shows whether an overdue notice was sent or couldn't be delivered. It is the screen the neighbor extends and returns loans from. - Who: a neighbor, opening their own My Loans screen. - **Whose loans it shows:** the loans of the neighbor asking. Sign-in, handled outside the model, supplies the neighbor's ID, resolving a person to their newest account, or to their closed one only when they have no newer. - **Actions offered:** nothing on this screen records anything by itself. Its actions issue commands owned by other steps: - **Return** — offered on every listed loan . - **Extend** — offered while canExtend is yes (a rule, a rule).

- **Extend Loan.** Let a borrower keep a tool longer by extending their loan from My Loans. An extension moves the due date later by one of the tool's default checkout periods. It is refused while someone is waiting for the tool, once the loan is overdue, while the borrower is suspended, and once the tool has been retired. The borrower, from the My Loans screen, on one of their own loans that My Loans offers Extend on. Sign-in, handled outside the shed's events, supplies the acting neighbor's ID.

- **Return Tool.** Let a borrower bring a tool back and end their loan themselves, from My Loans. The tool is then back in the shed, for the next holder or for anyone to borrow. The return records whether it was late, which is what counts toward a neighbor being flagged. The borrower, from their own My Loans screen, for one of their own loans. My Loans offers Return on every loan it lists , including overdue loans, loans whose tool was retired while out, and loans of a suspended neighbor. The shed manager does not record returns; a tool that never comes back is recorded lost instead (Record Tool Lost).


## Chase an overdue loan

- **Overdue Loans To Notify.** The list of overdue loans whose borrowers have not yet been sent an overdue notice. The overdue notice gateway works through it, so each borrower with an overdue loan is emailed once, as soon as the loan is overdue. Each item carries everything the email needs, so the gateway looks nothing up elsewhere. - Who: the Overdue Notice Gateway, a system reaction in the Send Overdue Notice step. No person reads this list, and it has no screen. - **What the gateway does with it:** for each loan on the list, it hands one notice to the email provider and records Overdue Notice Sent. How often it checks the list is that step's concern.

- **Send Overdue Notice.** Tell a borrower by email that their loan is overdue, once per loan, so they return the tool. The shed records that the notice was handed to the email provider. That record shows the borrower was told, and stops a second notice. - **Trigger form:** internally triggered. The Overdue Notice Gateway, a translation to the email provider outside the shed, watches the Overdue Loans To Notify list (steps/overdue-loans-to- notify.md). No person sends notices; only the gateway issues Send Overdue Notice. - **When it reads:** the list fills when midnight passes in the shed's local time, not when an event arrives, so the gateway reads it every 5 minutes, at any hour. A loan is therefore noticed within 5 minutes of becoming overdue . - **What the gateway does, for each loan on the list:** 1. It issues `Send Overdue Notice` for the loan, with the content from the loan's item: borrowerEmail, borrowerName, toolName and dueDate. If the command is refused, or is a repeat, nothing is sent for that loan. 2. Each recorded Overdue Notice Sent is handed to the provider from the event itself, using the loan ID as the hand-off's key. The hand-off is driven by the recorded event, not by the list: if the hand-off call errors, or the process stops before the provider accepts, it is repeated with the same key (after a restart too) until the provider accepts it. This is an outbox, not a domain event. - **The email:** to the address recorded on the event, giving the borrower's name, the tool's name and the due date recorded on it, asking them to return the tool, and saying that an overdue loan blocks new checkouts and pickups (a rule, a rule). - **The email port's contract**, from the shed's house rules: the provider accepts a hand-off with a key, sends at most one email per key, makes its own delivery attempts (3 over about an hour), and sends one final failure report quoting the key, signed so the shed can trust it. The final failure is recorded by Record Overdue Notice Failure (steps/record-overdue-notice- failure.md).

- **Record Overdue Notice Failure.** Record that an overdue notice could not be delivered to the borrower, so the shed manager knows the neighbor wasn't told and can follow up in person, and the borrower's My Loans shows that the notice didn't reach them. Externally triggered. The email provider, outside the shed, sends a final failure report for an overdue notice the shed handed it. The Email Provider Failure Report translation turns that report into the Record Overdue Notice Failure command. No person records a failure, and there is no screen. What the report carries: the reference the shed gave the notice when it handed it over, which is the loan's ID, since each loan gets at most one overdue notice; and the reason, refused or bounced. Anything else in the report, such as the email address, is not recorded.

- **Undelivered Notices.** Shows the shed manager every email the shed couldn't deliver — overdue notices and pickup notices — so they can reach the neighbor another way. A neighbor who wasn't told their loan is overdue, or that a tool is waiting for them, doesn't stay unnoticed. An entry stays only while there is something to follow up. - Who: the shed manager, opening the Undelivered Notices screen. - It shows: one entry per failed overdue notice whose loan is still open, and one per failed pickup notice whose set-aside hasn't ended, built from the facts above. The screen offers no actions. Following up is the shed manager's job, in person; no email is tried again after its failure is recorded (Send Overdue Notice, a rule). - Which failure reports become events is decided by the two failure steps: a report that matches no notice the shed sent, or that arrives after the loan or set-aside has ended, is ignored there and never reaches this view.


## Hold a tool for pickup

- **Place Hold.** Let a neighbor get in line for a tool that isn't available to borrow right now, by placing a hold from the Tool Catalog. Holds on a tool are fulfilled in the order they were placed: when the tool is back in the shed, it is set aside for the neighbor at the front of the queue. A neighbor, from the Tool Catalog, on a tool that is checked out, set aside for a holder, or reserved (back in the shed with holds waiting, not yet set aside). The catalog is the same for every neighbor, so it offers Place Hold on every such tool to everyone; a neighbor who may not hold it is refused with a reason they can see. The shed manager doesn't place holds.

- **My Holds.** Shows a neighbor the holds they have, on the My Holds screen: where they stand in each tool's queue, and when a tool has been set aside for them, the date to collect it by and whether the pickup email reached them. It is the only screen that tells a neighbor a tool is waiting for them, and the screen they cancel holds from. - Who: a neighbor, opening their own My Holds screen. - **Whose holds it shows:** the holds of the neighbor asking. Sign-in, handled outside the model, supplies the neighbor's ID, resolving a person to their newest account, or to their closed one only when they have no newer. - **Actions offered:** nothing on this screen records anything by itself. Its one action issues a command owned by another step: - **Cancel** — offered on every listed hold . - A neighbor collects a set-aside tool with an ordinary checkout from the Tool Catalog, not from here.

- **Holds To Collect.** The hold collector's to-do list: every hold whose holder has checked the tool out and whose hold hasn't yet been recorded collected. The collector works through it, so a holder's own checkout ends their hold as a Holds fact (Hold Collected) and the views about holds never infer it from the loan. - **Consumed by:** the Hold Collector (Collect Hold). Internal: no ui. - **What it lists:** one row per hold that is live (placed, not cancelled, expired or collected) on a tool that its holder now has on loan. A row names the hold, its tool and holder, and the loan that collected it.

- **Collect Hold.** When a holder checks out the tool they hold, record that their hold was collected, so the hold ends as a Holds fact and the views about holds hear it from their own context. Checking out records only Tool Checked Out; Hold Collected is recorded separately, by this step. The system does this itself; no person collects a hold. What follows a collection is decided by the steps that read Hold Collected: the hold leaves My Holds, Holds To Fulfill's queue, Pickup Notices To Send and Pickups To Expire, and a failed pickup email for it leaves Undelivered Notices. - **Pattern:** automation. The Hold Collector, a system processor, watches the Holds To Collect list (steps/holds-to-collect.md). Only the Hold Collector issues Collect Hold; it is not exposed to neighbors or the shed manager. - **Triggering condition:** a hold is on the list: its holder has an open loan on its tool. - **When it acts:** the list changes only when a checkout lands, so the collector acts whenever the list changes, on each item that appears. - **What it does:** it issues `Collect Hold` for the item's hold and loan. If the command is refused or is a repeat, nothing is recorded and the processor moves on.

- **Cancel Hold.** Let a neighbor give up a hold they no longer want, from My Holds, so the tool goes to the next neighbor in the queue instead of waiting on them. A hold can be cancelled while it is waiting or while the tool is set aside for the holder, and a suspended neighbor can cancel too. What follows a cancellation is decided by the views and steps that read Hold Cancelled: - Every hold behind the cancelled one on that tool moves up one place (My Holds, Holds To Fulfill). - Cancelling a hold that is ready for pickup ends the set-aside. The tool passes to the next hold, or becomes available if none is waiting (Holds To Fulfill, Tool Catalog), the same as when a set-aside expires. - A cancelled hold gives up its place for good. The neighbor may place a new hold on the same tool, which joins the back of the queue (Place Hold, a rule: only a live hold counts). The holder, from the My Holds screen, on one of their own holds that hasn't ended. The shed manager doesn't cancel holds. Sign-in, handled outside the shed's events, supplies the acting neighbor's ID.

- **Holds To Fulfill.** The hold fulfiller's to-do list: every tool that is back in the shed, not set aside for anyone, with a hold waiting, and which hold is next in line for it. The hold fulfiller works through it, so a returned tool is set aside for the neighbor at the front of its queue. - Who: the Hold Fulfiller, a system reaction in the Fulfill Hold step. No person reads this list, and it has no screen. - **What the fulfiller does with it:** for each tool on the list, it issues Fulfill Hold for the front hold, which records Hold Fulfilled.

- **Fulfill Hold.** When a tool is back in the shed with holds waiting, set it aside for the neighbor at the front of the queue, so the tool goes to holders in the order they asked. The system does this itself; no person decides it. Returning a tool records only Tool Returned; Hold Fulfilled is recorded separately, by this step. What follows a fulfilment is decided by the steps that read Hold Fulfilled: the holder is emailed that the tool is ready (Send Pickup Notice, which doesn't hold up the fulfilment), My Holds shows it ready for pickup with its collect-by date, the Tool Catalog shows it set aside, and the set-aside lapses at the end of its collect-by date (Expire Hold). - **Pattern:** automation. The Hold Fulfiller, a system processor, watches the Holds To Fulfill list (steps/holds-to-fulfill.md). No person fulfils holds; only the Hold Fulfiller issues Fulfill Hold, and it is not exposed to neighbors or the shed manager. - **Triggering condition:** a tool is on the list: it is in the shed, not set aside for anyone, and at least one hold on it is waiting. Each item names the tool, the hold at the front of its queue, and that hold's holder. - **When it acts:** the list changes only when an event lands (a return, an expiry, a cancellation, a closure), so the Hold Fulfiller acts whenever the list changes, on each item that appears. - **What it does:** it issues `Fulfill Hold` for the item's hold. If the command is refused or is a repeat, nothing is recorded and the processor moves on; the list will show the tool again with its new front hold, if there is one.

- **Pickup Notices To Send.** The pickup notice gateway's to-do list: every tool set aside for a holder whose pickup email hasn't gone out yet, with everything the email needs. The gateway works through it, so each holder is emailed once, as soon as a tool is set aside for them. - Who: the Pickup Notice Gateway, a system reaction in the Send Pickup Notice step. No person reads this list, and it has no screen. - **What the gateway does with it:** for each hold on the list, it records Pickup Notice Sent, and the email is then handed to the provider keyed by the hold's ID.

- **Send Pickup Notice.** Email a neighbor when a tool they hold has been set aside for them, so they collect it before the end of its collect-by date. The shed records that the email was handed to the email provider, and My Holds shows the neighbor that it was sent. - **Trigger form:** internally triggered. The Pickup Notice Gateway, a translation to the email provider outside the shed, watches the Pickup Notices To Send list (steps/pickup-notices-to- send.md). No person sends pickup emails; only the gateway issues Send Pickup Notice. - **When it acts:** the list changes only when an event lands (Hold Fulfilled adds a hold), so the gateway acts whenever the list changes, at any hour . Unlike the overdue list, nothing joins this list just because time passes. - **What the gateway does, for each set-aside on the list:** 1. It issues `Send Pickup Notice` for the hold, with the content from the item: holderEmail, holderName, toolName and collectByDate. If the command is refused, or is a repeat, nothing is sent for that hold. 2. Each recorded Pickup Notice Sent is handed to the provider from the event itself, using the hold ID as the hand-off's key. The hand-off is driven by the recorded event, not by the list: if the hand-off call errors, or the process stops before the provider accepts, it is repeated with the same key (after a restart too) until the provider accepts it. This is an outbox, not a domain event. - **The email:** to the address recorded on the event, giving the holder's name, the tool's name and the collect-by date recorded on it, and saying that the hold expires and the tool passes to the next neighbor if it isn't collected by then (a rule, a rule). - **The email port's contract**, from the shed's house rules, is the same as for overdue notices: the provider accepts a hand-off with a key, sends at most one email per key, makes its own delivery attempts (3 over about an hour), and sends one final failure report quoting the key, signed so the shed can trust it. A final failure is recorded by Record Pickup Notice Failure (steps/record-pickup-notice-failure.md). The hold still shows as ready for pickup, and the pickup window runs as usual.

- **Record Pickup Notice Failure.** Record that a pickup email could not be delivered to the holder, so the shed manager can see the neighbor wasn't told their tool is ready and can let them know another way, and the holder's My Holds shows that the email didn't reach them. Externally triggered. The email provider, outside the shed, sends a final failure report for a pickup email the shed handed it. The Pickup Email Failure Report translation turns that report into the Record Pickup Notice Failure command. No person records a failure, and there is no screen. What the report carries: the reference the shed gave the email when it handed it over, which is the hold's ID, since each set-aside gets at most one pickup email; and the reason, refused or bounced. Anything else in the report, such as the email address, is not recorded. A report whose reference is a loan's ID is about an overdue notice: it belongs to Record Overdue Notice Failure, and this translation drops it without issuing a command.

- **Pickups To Expire.** The pickup expirer's to-do list: every tool currently set aside for a holder, with the date it must be collected by. The expirer works through the ones whose collect-by date has passed, so a set-aside the holder doesn't collect in time ends and the tool moves on to the next neighbor. - Who: the Pickup Expirer, a system processor in the Expire Hold step (steps/expire-hold.md). No person or screen reads this list. - **What the expirer does with it:** it reads the set-asides that are due to expire, and issues Expire Hold for each.

- **Expire Hold.** When a holder doesn't collect a set-aside tool by the end of its collect-by date, end their hold so the tool passes to the next neighbor in the queue, or becomes available to borrow. The system does this itself; no person decides it. Internally triggered. The Pickup Expirer, a system processor in this step, reads Pickups To Expire every 5 minutes, at any hour, and issues Expire Hold for each set-aside whose collect-by date has passed. A lapsed set-aside is therefore expired within about 5 minutes of midnight, shed time. No person expires a hold; the shed manager doesn't cancel set-asides by hand. There is no screen.


## Keep late returners in check

- **Neighbors To Flag.** The late-return flagger's to-do list: every neighbor whose late returns have reached the threshold (3 within 90 days) and who hasn't yet been flagged for them, with the loans those late returns belong to, each with the tool's name, the due date it missed and the date it came back. The flagger works through it, so the shed manager sees each of these neighbors flagged on Neighbors and decides whether to suspend them. - Who: the Late Return Flagger, a system processor in the Flag Neighbor step (steps/flag-neighbor.md). No person or screen reads this list. - **What the flagger does with it:** for each neighbor returned, it issues Flag Neighbor naming the loans returned, which the flag uses up.

- **Flag Neighbor.** When a neighbor's late returns reach the late-return threshold, record that they crossed it, carrying the late returns behind the flag, so the shed manager sees them on Neighbors and decides whether to suspend them. The system flags; a person decides. Internally triggered. The Late Return Flagger, a system processor in this step, acts whenever a neighbor appears on Neighbors To Flag, and issues Flag Neighbor for them with the loans that list gives. It is driven by events, not a schedule: a neighbor joins that list when a late return is recorded. No person flags a neighbor, and there is no screen. The neighbor isn't told; a flag is a prompt for the shed manager, not a penalty.

- **Suspend Neighbor.** Let the shed manager suspend any active neighbor, so someone who keeps returning tools late stops borrowing until the shed manager reinstates them. Usually the neighbor has been flagged for late returns. The system flags; the shed manager decides. The shed manager issues Suspend Neighbor from the Neighbors screen, for any active neighbor, flagged or not. The usual case is a neighbor the system has flagged for late returns, whose flags and late returns the screen shows; it also covers a neighbor who closed their account while suspended and signed up again with a different email. The system never suspends anyone: reaching the late-return threshold only flags a neighbor. (Version 1 drew this trigger from a separate Flagged Neighbors screen; v2 folds that screen into Neighbors.)

- **Reinstate Neighbor.** Let the shed manager lift a neighbor's suspension, so the neighbor can borrow again with a clean late-return record. The shed manager, from the Neighbors screen, for a neighbor shown there as suspended. A suspension never lifts by itself after a set time. (Version 1 drew this trigger from a separate Suspended Neighbors screen; v2 folds that screen into Neighbors.)


## Take a tool or a neighbor out of the shed

- **Retire Tool.** Take a tool out of the shed for good: a damaged tool, one the shed no longer keeps, or one added with a mistake. The shed manager retires it from Tool Inventory, and neighbors can no longer borrow or hold it. Retiring is final: nothing un-retires a tool. A tool retired to correct a mistake in its details is added again as a new tool. What follows a retirement is decided by the views and steps that read Tool Retired: - The tool drops off the Tool Catalog at once . It drops off Tool Inventory at once if it is in the shed. A tool retired while on loan stays on Tool Inventory, shown as retired and out, until it is returned or recorded lost , so the shed manager can still record it lost if it never comes back. - Every hold on the tool ends, waiting or set aside: Retire Tool records Hold Cancelled for each in the same decision. No hold is passed on, and the holders are not told; their holds drop off My Holds . - A loan on the tool carries on, can't be extended , and ends when the tool is returned or recorded lost. - Check Out Tool and Place Hold refuse a retired tool. A tool lost or broken by a borrower, and never returned, is not retired here. The shed manager records it lost against the loan (Record Tool Lost), which ends the loan and retires the tool in one step. A lost tool that later turns up is added again as a new tool. The shed manager, from the Tool Inventory screen, which offers Retire on every listed tool not already retired . Sign-in, handled outside the shed's events, supplies the shed-manager role.

- **Record Tool Lost.** Let the shed manager record that a tool out on loan is lost or broken and won't come back. Doing so ends the loan, so the borrower isn't left blocked by a tool they can't return, and takes the tool out of the shed for good, ending any holds on it. The shed manager, from the Tool Inventory screen, on a tool shown as out on loan ("mark lost", a rule). That includes a tool retired while out. The borrower doesn't record a loss, and the shed manager doesn't record a return on the borrower's behalf instead.

- **Close Account.** Let a neighbor who is moving away or no longer wants to take part close their own account, from My Account. Closing is the neighbor's own choice, and it is final: nothing reopens, suspends or reinstates a closed account. A neighbor who wants to come back signs up again as a new account. Closing records Neighbor Account Closed and, for each of the neighbor's holds, Hold Cancelled; what follows is decided by the views and steps that read them: - All of the neighbor's holds end, each by its Hold Cancelled. Holds waiting behind theirs move up, and a tool set aside for them passes to the next hold, or becomes available if none is waiting, as when a set-aside expires (My Holds, Holds To Fulfill, Tool Catalog). - The neighbor can still view My Account, read-only, and can't borrow, extend or place holds (My Account, Check Out Tool, Extend Loan, Place Hold). - A closed account is never flagged. The Neighbors screen shows it as closed, with no action and no open flags (Neighbors To Flag, Neighbors). - If the account was suspended when it was closed, its email can't be used to sign up again (Register Neighbor, a rule). Otherwise it can. The neighbor, from the My Account screen, on their own active or suspended account. My Account offers Close Account on any account that isn't closed. While the neighbor has a tool on loan, the closing is refused with a reason they can see . The shed manager doesn't close accounts. Sign-in, handled outside the shed's events, supplies the acting neighbor's ID. It resolves a person to their newest account, and to a closed one only when there is no newer.
